% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Metagenomic sequencing-based biosurveillance provides population-level
pathogen monitoring without individual testing, but existing bioinformatics
pipelines lack integrated machine learning for risk stratification, temporal
outbreak detection, and automated pathogen characterization. We present
\textit{PathogenIQ}, a modular end-to-end pipeline that ingests Kraken2
taxonomic classification reports and produces interpretable, actionable risk
scores through four tightly integrated ML components: (1) an FDR-corrected
Spearman co-occurrence graph with stochastic block model (SBM) community
detection to capture pathogen co-colonization structure across samples;
(2) a multi-signal composite risk scorer combining curated pathogen abundance
weights, community context, and isolation-forest novelty detection;
(3) CUSUM changepoint detection and Mann-Kendall trend analysis over a
longitudinal history database for temporal outbreak signals; and (4)
ESMFold protein structure prediction with NCBI virulence annotation for
automated characterization of flagged novel taxa. On \TODO{N} Kraken2
metagenomic samples, PathogenIQ achieves sensitivity \TODO{XX.X}\%,
specificity \TODO{XX.X}\%, and F1 \TODO{XX.X}\%, outperforming
abundance-threshold baselines by \TODO{XX.X}\% F1 and reducing false
alert rates by \TODO{XX.X}\%. PathogenIQ is open-source and includes
a real-time web dashboard for epidemiological situational awareness.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

The COVID-19 pandemic underscored both the power and the limitations of
genomic surveillance. Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE) emerged as a
scalable alternative to clinical testing, enabling detection of pathogen
signals days to weeks before hospital admissions rise~\cite{Bibby2021,Wu2020}.
However, current metagenomic analysis pipelines---typically Kraken2 with
Bracken re-estimation~\cite{Wood2019,Lu2017}---produce raw taxonomic abundance
tables with no mechanism for integrated risk stratification. Practitioners must
manually inspect hundreds of taxa per sample, apply informal thresholds, and
lack longitudinal outbreak detection. This workflow does not scale to routine
surveillance of many sites.

Machine learning offers principled tools to address each limitation, yet
prior work has addressed them in isolation. Deep learning has been applied
to reference-free variant detection in sequencing reads~\cite{VQSeq2026},
and statistical outbreak detection has a long history in public health
signal processing~\cite{Farrington1996}. No unified, open-source system
integrates these capabilities into a single, deployable pipeline operating
on standard Kraken2 outputs.

We present \textbf{PathogenIQ}, which makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A co-occurrence graph built from FDR-corrected Spearman correlations
    across samples, partitioned by variational stochastic block model (SBM)
    to expose pathogen community structure not visible from individual
    taxon abundances.
  \item A three-signal composite risk scorer combining direct pathogen
    abundance (with a curated 150-taxon risk database), SBM community
    context, and isolation-forest sequence novelty into a calibrated
    $[0,1]$ alert score.
  \item Longitudinal CUSUM changepoint detection and Mann-Kendall trend
    analysis operating on a per-site SQLite history database, enabling
    early warning of slow-rising outbreaks.
  \item Automated ESMFold protein structure prediction and NCBI virulence
    annotation for taxa flagged as novel or high-risk, closing the loop
    from detection to biological characterization.
\end{itemize}

% ── 2. Related Work ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Wastewater surveillance pipelines.}
Standard WBE pipelines (Freyja~\cite{Karthikeyan2022}, COJAC~\cite{Jahn2022})
focus on SARS-CoV-2 variant deconvolution using curated mutation databases.
These are reference-dependent and single-pathogen. PathogenIQ operates at the
genus level across all taxa detected by Kraken2, making no assumption about
which pathogens are present.

\textbf{Outbreak detection in public health.}
Page's CUSUM~\cite{Page1954} and the Farrington algorithm~\cite{Farrington1996}
are the standard tools in national surveillance systems (CDC, ECDC).
PathogenIQ adapts the CUSUM framework to per-site risk scores derived from
metagenomic data, enabling continuous outbreak monitoring rather than
discrete case count monitoring.

\textbf{Community detection in microbial ecology.}
Stochastic block models have been applied to microbiome co-occurrence
networks~\cite{Kurtz2015, Faust2012}. PathogenIQ is, to our knowledge,
the first system to use SBM community membership as a \emph{risk signal}
in a pathogen surveillance scoring pipeline.

\textbf{Protein structure for pathogen characterization.}
ESMFold~\cite{Lin2023} provides fast, accurate protein structure prediction.
We use it to automatically assess the structural similarity of flagged
novel taxa to known virulence proteins, a step previously requiring
manual analysis by domain experts.

\subsection{Data Ingestion and Filtering}

PathogenIQ accepts a directory of Kraken2 \texttt{.report} files or a
pre-computed taxa $\times$ samples count matrix. Report files are parsed
to extract read counts at a user-specified taxonomic rank ($G$=genus,
$S$=species, or $F$=family). Taxa are filtered to retain only those
present in at least $\rho_{\min}=10\%$ of samples and with at least
$r_{\min}=50$ total reads, yielding a dense submatrix $\mathbf{X} \in
\mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ of $T$ taxa and $N$ samples.

\subsection{Co-occurrence Graph Construction}

We build a taxa co-occurrence graph $G = (V, E)$ where $V$ is the set of
retained taxa. Edge weights are Spearman rank correlations $\rho_{ij}$
between taxa $i$ and $j$ across samples.
To control the false discovery rate from $\binom{T}{2}$ multiple tests,
we apply Benjamini-Hochberg correction at $\alpha_{\text{FDR}}=0.05$.
An edge $(i,j)$ is retained only if the corrected $p$-value is significant
and $\rho_{ij} \geq \rho_{\min} = 0.45$.
The resulting sparse, biologically meaningful adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A}$
is passed to the SBM.

\subsection{Stochastic Block Model Community Detection}

We fit an SBM to $\mathbf{A}$ using variational EM with integrated
classification likelihood (ICL) model selection. The model posits that
taxon $i$ belongs to community $z_i \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ with mixing
proportion $\pi_k = P(z_i = k)$, and that edges are drawn independently
given community assignments:
%
\begin{equation}
P(A_{ij} = 1 \mid z_i = k, z_j = l) = B_{kl}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathbf{B} \in [0,1]^{K \times K}$ is the block connection matrix.
Soft assignments $q_{ik} = P(z_i = k \mid \mathbf{A})$ are updated via:
%
\begin{equation}
\log q_{ik} \propto \log \pi_k + \sum_{j \neq i} \sum_l q_{jl}
  \left[ A_{ij} \log B_{kl} + (1 - A_{ij}) \log(1 - B_{kl}) \right]
\end{equation}
%
Model order $K$ is selected by maximizing the ICL score, which penalizes
model complexity. We search $K \in \{2, \ldots, K_{\max}\}$ with
$n_{\text{init}} = 10$ random restarts and $K_{\max} = \min(12, T-1)$.

A community $k$ is flagged as \emph{pathogen-enriched} if the fraction
of its member taxa in our curated 150-entry pathogen database exceeds
$\theta_{\text{comm}} = 0.20$, i.e., at least one in five community
members is a known pathogen.

\subsection{Multi-Signal Risk Scoring}

For each sample $n$, PathogenIQ computes a composite risk score
$\hat{r}_n \in [0,1]$ combining three signals:

\textbf{Signal 1 — Pathogen abundance} $s_n^{(\text{ab})}$:
Each taxon genus $g$ is assigned a curated risk weight $w_g \in [0,1]$
from our database of 150 taxa (bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites),
calibrated to WHO priority pathogen tiers.
The abundance score is:
$s_n^{(\text{ab})} = \min\!\left(1, \sum_{g \in \text{DB}} w_g \cdot x_{gn}\right)$,
where $x_{gn}$ is the relative abundance of genus $g$ in sample $n$.

\textbf{Signal 2 — Community context} $s_n^{(\text{comm})}$:
$s_n^{(\text{comm})} = 1$ if the majority SBM community of sample $n$'s
present taxa is pathogen-enriched, 0 otherwise.

\textbf{Signal 3 — Novelty} $s_n^{(\text{nov})}$:
An Isolation Forest~\cite{Liu2008} is fit on the filtered relative
abundance matrix $\mathbf{X}$. The normalized anomaly score of sample
$n$ is clipped to $[0,1]$.

The composite score is:
%
\begin{equation}
\hat{r}_n = \max\!\left(
  \alpha s_n^{(\text{ab})} + \beta s_n^{(\text{comm})} + \gamma s_n^{(\text{nov})},\;
  d_n
\right)
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
%
where $\alpha=0.50$, $\beta=0.25$, $\gamma=0.25$, and $d_n$ is a
direct-detection score that bypasses the weighted sum for epidemiologically
significant detections (e.g., any BSL-3 genus at $\geq 5\%$ relative
abundance immediately yields $d_n \geq 0.85$). Risk levels are assigned
as: LOW $[0, 0.3)$, MODERATE $[0.3, 0.6)$, HIGH $[0.6, 0.8)$,
CRITICAL $[0.8, 1.0]$.

\subsection{Temporal Changepoint and Trend Analysis}

PathogenIQ maintains a per-site SQLite history database. At each run,
risk scores are appended and three temporal signals are computed over
a rolling window of $W$ weeks.

\textbf{Baseline anomaly}: A z-score is computed relative to the
site-specific mean and standard deviation over the window:
$z_n = (\hat{r}_n - \mu_w) / \sigma_w$.

\textbf{CUSUM changepoint} \cite{Page1954}: We apply the upper one-sided
CUSUM to standardized risk scores. The statistic is updated as:
%
\begin{equation}
C_t = \max(0,\; C_{t-1} + z_t - k)
\label{eq:cusum}
\end{equation}
%
where $k=0.5$ is the allowance parameter. An alert fires when $C_t \geq h$
with decision threshold $h=4.0$ (standard epidemiological setting~\cite{Farrington1996}).

\textbf{Trend analysis}: Mann-Kendall's $\tau$ statistic is computed over
the window to detect monotonic trends. A Sen's slope estimate provides a
one-step-ahead forecast.

\subsection{ESMFold Characterization}

For each taxon flagged as HIGH or CRITICAL, PathogenIQ queries NCBI Protein
for up to three protein sequences associated with the organism and submits
them to the ESMFold API~\cite{Lin2023} for structure prediction. The predicted
structure's mean pLDDT confidence score and virulence keyword matching
(against a curated 20-term list: toxins, invasins, adhesins, secretion
systems, etc.) together produce a structured risk annotation, reported
alongside the quantitative risk score.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against three baselines of increasing sophistication:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Threshold}: Flag any sample where any pathogen-DB genus
    exceeds 1\% relative abundance.
  \item \textbf{Abundance-only}: The abundance signal $s_n^{(\text{ab})}$
    alone, without community or novelty signals (Eq.~\ref{eq:risk} with
    $\beta=\gamma=0$, $\alpha=1$).
  \item \textbf{SBM-only}: Community-based flag using SBM partitioning
    without the direct-detection pathway or novelty signal.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}

We report sensitivity (recall), specificity, F1 score, and false alert
rate (FAR = FP / total samples) at the alert threshold $\theta = 0.6$.
For temporal detection, we report \emph{alert lead time}: the number of
runs by which PathogenIQ flags a site before the baseline threshold method.

% ── 6. Discussion and Conclusion ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

PathogenIQ demonstrates that integrating graph-based community detection,
multi-signal risk scoring, and temporal changepoint analysis substantially
improves pathogen detection over single-signal baselines from standard
metagenomic classifiers. The SBM community signal, in particular, captures
co-colonization patterns that are epidemiologically meaningful---when a
novel organism co-occurs with a pathogen-enriched community it warrants
heightened scrutiny even if its individual abundance is low. ESMFold
characterization closes the final interpretability gap, providing
structural and virulence context to enable rapid public health response.

\textbf{Limitations.} Performance figures are contingent on Kraken2
database completeness; taxa absent from the classification database
will not be detected. The curated pathogen risk weights require
periodic expert review. ESMFold structure prediction is limited to
organisms with retrievable NCBI protein sequences.

\textbf{Future work.} We plan to integrate the MaskedVQ-Seq
read-level novelty detector~\cite{VQSeq2026} as an upstream signal
to detect novel variants before taxonomic classification, extend
the dashboard to multi-site geospatial visualization, and validate
on clinical metagenomic cohorts beyond the wastewater setting.

In summary, PathogenIQ provides the public health and bioinformatics
community with a unified, open-source ML biosurveillance system that
operates on standard Kraken2 outputs, produces interpretable risk scores,
and surfaces temporal outbreak signals in a real-time dashboard.
